mpvRx includes an audio-focused library and now-playing experience in addition to video playback.

:::note
The ExoPlayer features described below are part of the `experimental/exoplayer-audio`
branch. They may not be present in your installed stable release. Device-dependent
output support is not guaranteed by the presence of a setting.
:::

## Audio engines

Open **Settings > Audio > Audio engine** to choose the engine for audio-only
items. Open or reload a track after changing the choice. Video playback continues
to use mpv.

| Engine | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| ExoPlayer | Uses Android Media3 for audio, with the processing, transition and streaming controls below. |
| mpv | Uses native mpv playback and its supported audio configuration, filters and scripts. |

ExoPlayer never automatically switches to mpv because a decoder fails or because
`mpv.conf` contains audio filters. Native `af` filters do not apply to ExoPlayer.
For a source that requires native decoding or scripts, choose mpv explicitly and
reopen it. Audio-delay and external-audio-track controls remain mpv-specific.

Use the player's **Audio** sheet to select embedded tracks and reach its available
output controls and equalizer. Audio properties identify the active engine and
the source, decoder and output formats.

### Gapless and crossfade

Set **Crossfade** in Audio settings or the player's Audio sheet:

- **0 seconds (Gapless):** queues the prepared next item on the same ExoPlayer
  timeline, without overlapping the tracks.
- **1-12 seconds:** blends eligible tracks using an equal-power fade. Short tracks
  can use a shorter overlap than the configured duration.

Automatic transitions require a next item and permission to advance the queue:
enable **Autoplay next audio**, or use repeat-all. Repeat-one, an A-B loop, Cast or
Syncplay can prevent a prepared transition. Crossfade is not used for audiobooks,
live/non-seekable streams, multichannel/protected output or active spatialization.

Pause suspends both sides of a fade. If a player buffers, the fade waits rather
than advancing its volume envelope without audible playback. Seeking, changing
the queue or changing relevant output settings cancels obsolete preparation.
Gapless playback still depends on the source, decoder and next item being ready;
a failed preload can require normal sequential loading.

### Processing and output rate

| Control | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Equalizer and boost | Five-band EQ and digital gain for compatible PCM output. |
| Skip silence | Uses Media3 to skip silent passages. It is off by default and unavailable on protected or multichannel output. |
| ReplayGain: Track | Uses the embedded track-gain tag and peak information when available. |
| ReplayGain: Album | Uses album gain when present, otherwise track gain. Files without usable tags receive no tag-based gain. |
| Volume normalization | Adjusts loudness dynamically instead of reading ReplayGain tags. Selecting ReplayGain disables this setting; enabling normalization disables ReplayGain. |
| Dynamic range compression | Reduces the difference between loud and quiet passages on compatible PCM output. |
| Output sample rate | Auto, device rate, 44.1 kHz, 48 kHz or 96 kHz for compatible PCM playback. |

PCM processing and crossfade can require hardware offload to be disabled.
Protected or multichannel output bypasses incompatible processing instead of
silently downmixing the source. Disabled controls reflect that output restriction,
not a missing preference.

:::warning
Volume above 100% applies digital gain. Peak protection is not a reason to use
unlimited boost: reduce gain if you hear distortion or pumping.
:::

### Streaming quality and cache

**Unmetered audio quality** and **Metered audio quality** set separate bitrate
ceilings. Android's metered-network classification determines which applies;
Wi-Fi can also be metered. **Original quality** removes the configured ceiling.
These settings choose among available representations, not arbitrary transcoding
rates. A single-format stream may have no lower-bitrate alternative.

**Cache streamed audio** stores a bounded amount of streamed media in private
app cache storage. Adaptive manifests are fetched fresh, and cache errors allow
network reads to continue. Cached bytes may be evicted and do not guarantee
complete offline playback. Use [Downloads](/docs/library-and-streaming/downloads-and-online-quality)
for files you need to keep.

Transient network failures receive bounded retries. Seekable sources resume from
the last known position; live or non-seekable sources restart instead of resuming
an old timestamp.
Supported webpage sources use the existing yt-dlp runtime to resolve a fresh
audio URL on load or retry. Authentication errors, unavailable formats and
unsupported sites can still fail without switching engines.

## Dolby Atmos and spatial audio

### Dolby Atmos

For a loaded Dolby track, the player shows the double-D icon immediately below
**Now Playing**. Confirmed E-AC-3 JOC metadata displays **Dolby Atmos**; other
detected Dolby codecs display **Dolby Audio**. The indicator identifies the
current source format, remains visible while that track is paused, and is not
enabled merely by turning on the Atmos preference. It does not certify the
device's rendering path.

**Prefer Dolby Atmos** is available when Android reports a Dolby Digital Plus
JOC decoder or a connected output advertising E-AC-3 JOC. The player's Audio sheet
shows the corresponding toggle when that capability is reported.

This is a track preference, not a stereo-to-Atmos effect or a device-wide Dolby
switch. It can prefer an available Atmos representation but cannot add one to a
source. A compatible decoder alone is not proof that the output route renders
the full Atmos mix.

Check Audio properties for the actual path: JOC passthrough, decoded JOC,
E-AC-3 core-only output, or unverified rendering. Devices that decode only the
E-AC-3 core should not be treated as verified Atmos output.

### Android spatial audio

**Spatial audio** opts mpvRx into Android's Spatializer on supported Android 12L
(API 32) and newer devices. Availability also depends on the current output route,
headphones, system settings and source format.

If the current route supports spatialization but Android has disabled it, tapping
the player's spatial-audio control opens Android sound settings. The app cannot
force the system switch on. It also does not synthesize an Atmos mix from stereo.

## Music library

The Music source selector offers local storage, Jellyfin, Navidrome and
audiobooks. Local music includes songs, albums, artists, playlists and folders,
with artwork-aware layouts, search, sorting, favorites, folder exclusions and
a minimum-track-duration filter.

Changing source replaces the browsed catalog; it does not merge remote and local
albums. If a server is unavailable, switch back to Local rather than deleting
its configuration. See [Jellyfin and Navidrome](/docs/library-and-streaming/jellyfin-and-navidrome).

### Playlists and saved queue

**Play Next** inserts after the current item; **Add to Queue** appends. You can
also save the queue as a named playlist. Favorites remain synchronized with the
active music notification.

Eligible ExoPlayer sessions save the queue, shuffle order, current item, position,
speed, volume and pause state atomically in private, non-backed-up storage when
**Recently Played** history retention is enabled. A saved queue is a playback
snapshot, not a download or a named playlist.

**Resume queue** in Android Auto and Play on the music widget can reopen that
snapshot after process recreation while ExoPlayer is selected. This is an explicit
resume action, not automatic playback at app launch. Missing files, revoked file
permissions, expired credentials and unavailable sources still require attention.
Disabling history retention clears the saved queue. See
[Playlists, queues, and history](/docs/library-and-streaming/playlists-and-history).

## Android Auto and widget

Android Auto and authorized media-browser clients can browse local songs, audio
playlists, recents, downloaded audio and the current/saved queue. Long track lists
are paged; voice search looks for local songs by title or artist. This is not a
full Jellyfin or Navidrome discovery interface.

Add **mpvRx music** from the Android launcher's widget picker. It resizes
horizontally and provides artwork, title/artist, previous, Play/Pause and next.
Tapping the metadata opens mpvRx. Transport actions use the existing playback
service rather than a second player.

Media-access permissions, service/notification settings and Android background
restrictions still apply. The widget cannot play a saved queue when no eligible
snapshot exists or history retention is disabled.

## Download metadata

Completed yt-dlp audio downloads can embed title, artist, album and artwork using
the optional Mutagen dependency installed with the yt-dlp runtime. Supported
containers include MP3, M4A, FLAC and Ogg/Opus. Artwork must be available in a
supported format. Tagging uses a temporary copy and never requires transcoding
the audio. Unsupported containers and tagging failures leave the downloaded file
intact. See [Downloads and online quality](/docs/library-and-streaming/downloads-and-online-quality).

## Lyrics

mpvRx can display embedded or local lyrics and fetch online lyrics from LRCLIB. When several sources are available, switch sources from the lyrics view. Availability and timing quality depend on the track metadata and provider result.

Timed lyrics follow playback; plain text remains readable without synchronization.
Changing tracks cancels in-flight loading and source conversion so a slow result
from the previous song cannot replace the current lyrics. Optional translation
and Indic-script romanization remain best-effort transformations; keep the
original source available for comparison.

## Visualizers

Blob, Galaxy, Cuboid, and Particle modes are available, along with an optional audio-reactive seekbar. They are presentation choices, not audio processors, and do not change the decoded signal.

With ExoPlayer, visualization uses an eligible audio-session capture path. It can
be unavailable during offloaded or protected output; recording permission and
device support also affect capture.

Galaxy and Particle emphasize many moving elements; Cuboid emphasizes geometry;
Blob uses a continuous organic surface. All use additional rendering resources,
so disable them when battery life, PiP, or thermal headroom is the priority.

## Background playback

Audio and video share the app's playback session but have separate background
playback settings. Enable audio background playback when you want music to
continue after leaving the player. Notification re-entry and mini-player controls
operate on the same live queue, without starting another engine.

See [Orientation, background playback, and PiP](/docs/playback/orientation-background-and-pip)
for handoff behavior and Android permission considerations.
